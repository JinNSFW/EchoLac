# Research evidence: Vietnamese learners' English pronunciation errors (collected 2026-11-22)

Collected by two Haiku/high research subagents from web search (abstracts/snippets; small samples). [research] = empirical study; [teaching] = guide/blog only; [U] = unverified. Treat figures as provisional.

## Vietnamese baseline
- Syllable C(w)V(C). Native codas: /p t k/ (always unreleased), /m n ŋ/, glides /w j/ (sources say 6 or 8 codas) [14,18]. No clusters [1,14]. No affricates [6]. No native /θ ð ʃ ʒ tʃ dʒ/ [3,5,6]. Final /s z f v l/ absent in standard analyses [/l/ U].
- Syllable-timed, lexical tone, no phonemic vowel length [10,11]. Glottal stop onset before vowel-initial syllables [B7].

## Segmental errors (rank by intelligibility impact from research agent)
1. Final stop omission / unreleasing /t d k ɡ b p/: "cat" → [kæ̚]/[kæ]. [1] omission most frequent [U n]; [8] n=22: unreleased/glottalised 49.3% of coda-stop variants [research, abstract]. HIGH.
2. Final /s z/ omission (plural, 3sg, possessive): "books" → [bʊk]. [2]=[B2] Disney & Le 2024, n=16 monologues: 28.4% omitted; after consonant 50.7%; grammatical -s omitted more than lexical; frequency correlates [research]. HIGH.
3. Vowel length/quality pairs /ɪ–iː/, /ʊ–uː/, /ɒ–ɔː/: "ship/sheep" both [ʃiːp]. [10] VNU FS teaching study; [11] HCMIU thesis n=20 (acoustics: quality differences more than length) [research]. HIGH.
4. Consonant clusters onset & final: "street" → [siːt] / [sətriːt]; deletion favoured over epenthesis for onsets [4] CTU 2022 n=39, [13]; final voiceless-plosive clusters worst [4]; 5 repair strategies (OT, n=7) [15] [research]. HIGH.
5. -ed endings: "asked" → [æsk], "played" → [pleɪ]; Hai Phong Univ n=31 (2024), n=32 (2022): /ɪd/ easiest [research, U URL]; Can Tho HS n=31 [U]. HIGH.
6. /θ ð/: /θ/ → /t/ ("think" → tɪŋk; "bath" → bɑːt), /ð/ → /z/ or /d/ ("this" → zɪs/dɪs). [3] Bui 2016 IJAL, 10 recordings + 115 questionnaire [research]. HIGH.
7. /ʃ ʒ tʃ dʒ/ vs /s z/: "ship" → sɪp, "job" → zɒp/dʒɒp; [5] Luu 2011, [6] Nu 2009, [7] thesis n=58 minimal pairs /tʃ–dʒ/, /s–ʃ/ [research]. HIGH.
8. Final devoicing /d z b ɡ v/: "bed" → bɛt, "dog" → dɒk [1],[12] [research, U n]. MEDIUM.
9. /æ/ vs /ɛ/ and /ʌ/: "cat" → kɛt; "cut" → kɒt [U; teaching]. MEDIUM-HIGH.
10. Schwa / vowel reduction absent: full vowels in unstressed syllables ("photograph" with 4 full vowels). [9] Ingram & Nguyen 2004 SST: natives reduce more (p=0.04) [research]. MEDIUM.
11. /r/ realisation (Northern trill/[z]-like, Southern velar/approximant) and dialect /z j v/: Northern d/gi/r → /z/; Southern d/gi/v → /j/ ("very" → jɛri); /v/–/w/ [teaching 17]. MEDIUM.
12. Final /l/ dropped or → /n/, /w/; /l/–/n/ confusion in some Northern speakers: "ball" → bɔːn/bɔː [U]. MEDIUM.
13. Initial /p/ (Vietnamese lacks native initial /p/) → /b/; unaspirated /p t k/ [U]. MEDIUM-LOW.
14. Diphthongs reduced: /eɪ/ → [e] ("day" → dɛ), /oʊ/ → [o], final glide dropped [teaching, U]. LOW-MEDIUM.
15. /ŋ h ɡ–k w/: no reliable study; low risk. LOW.
Dialect: Central (Huế, Đà Nẵng, Quảng Nam) keeps tr/ch, s/x, d/gi/r distinctions so /z/–/j/ less problematic [teaching]; reported v/d merging [U]. Central tones: 5 non-stopped [U].

## Suprasegmental / connected speech
1. Word stress misplacement (hoTEL, comPUter, deVElopment): Duong Minh Tuan 2018 n=45 [B3] [research]; Hahn 2004 [B13]: correct primary stress improves recall/ratings [research]. HIGH.
2. Compound vs phrase stress (GREENhouse vs green HOUSE): Nguyen & Ingram 2002 [B6] — advanced learners match pitch/intensity but not timing [research]. MEDIUM.
3. Suffix stress shifts (-tion, -ic, -ity), noun/verb pairs (REcord/reCORD): [teaching]. MEDIUM.
4. Weak forms / no vowel reduction in function words (to, of, and, can, for): [research, non-Vietnamese tonal L1]; Vietnamese gap. HIGH to teach.
5. Rhythm: syllable-timed staccato; Ingram & Pittam 1991 [B5]: less consistent syllable durations; Pittam & Ingram 1991 [B4]: tonal interference with stress, sắc tone singled out [research]. HIGH.
6. Nuclear/contrastive stress: Nguyen, Ingram & Pensalfini 2008 System 36 [U]. MEDIUM.
7. Intonation tonal transfer: Nguyễn & Đào 2018 [B1] (20 Southern learners vs 10 Australians): beginners' tunes follow Vietnamese tones; advanced approximate native [research]. Ngô Phương Anh 2017 [B8] n=27: training improved tonicity & tone [research]. Yes/no rise vs wh fall. HIGH.
8. Connected speech: Nguyen & Ingram 2004 [B7]: natives linked ~4.5× more; Vietnamese inserted glottal stops before vowels and gaps where linking should occur, even advanced [research]. HIGH.
9. Elision/assimilation (wanna, gotta, next day): review [B17] [teaching]. MEDIUM.

## Teaching techniques (evidence)
- HVPT: Uchihara, Karas & Thomson 2025 meta-analysis (79 studies) medium–large perception gains; production transfer mixed [B9] [research]. Use multiple voices for perception drills.
- Minimal-pair perception then production [teaching / weak research].
- Shadowing: review [B10] improves comprehensibility/intelligibility [research]; good for connected speech.
- Visual pitch feedback: Taniguchi & Abberton [B11]; Min & Pak 2007 [B12] [research]: pitch contour better than spectrogram; aim for pattern-matching not mimicry.
- Priority by intelligibility: Munro & Derwing 1995 [B14]; Levis 2005 [B15] Intelligibility Principle; Functional load; Jenkins 2000 Lingua Franca Core [B16]: core = consonants (except θ/ð arguably non-core in LFC!), cluster simplification avoidance (no initial deletion), vowel length, nuclear stress; word stress NOT core. (Note: in LFC, /θ ð/ substitutions are acceptable for ELF intelligibility — EchoLạc may still teach them but at lower priority than finals/clusters.)
- Back-chaining for clusters ("street": eet → reet → treet → street), tapping stress: [teaching].

## Auto-detectability in browser (phoneme recogniser wav2vec2 espeak + Web Audio pitch/energy)
- Reliable-ish: phoneme omission/substitution for segmentals (final consonants, -s/-ed, clusters, θ/ð, sibilants, vowel quality), with confidence threshold; pauses/phrase grouping (VAD); phrase-final F0 direction (rise vs fall); stress location by relative energy+duration+pitch over syllables.
- Low confidence (soft feedback only): vowel length per se (model labels iː vs ɪ by quality), schwa in weak forms, linking/glottal onset, elision. Dialect: do not detect.
- Note: espeak IPA tokens for English used by model: p b t d k ɡ f v θ ð s z ʃ ʒ h tʃ dʒ m n ŋ l ɹ w j; vowels iː ɪ ɛ æ ɑː ɔː ʊ uː ʌ ə ɚ ɜː eɪ aɪ ɔɪ oʊ aʊ; extra tokens ɾ ᵻ əl ɑːɹ ɔːɹ ɪɹ ɛɹ ʊɹ aɪɚ aɪə i.

## Sequence suggested by research agent
- A1–A2: final consonants & -s in short words; θ/ð & sibilants in common words; 2–3 syllable word stress with Đà Nẵng vocab; statement fall; phrase pausing.
- B1: clusters, -ed, vowel pairs; compound vs phrase stress (Marble Mountains, Han River); yes/no vs wh; linking C+V; shadowing short chunks.
- B2: weak forms, nuclear/contrastive stress, rhythm, elision/assimilation, -ed clusters.
- C1: focus/information structure, tone vs tonicity in extended speech, attitudinal intonation, self-monitoring.
